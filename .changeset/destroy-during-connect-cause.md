The reference now says what `connect()` rejects with when `destroy()` runs while it is still connecting, and how long a TLS server waits for a handshake. Over TCP or TLS, before or during the handshake, `connect()` rejects with an `MllpConnectionError` whose `phase` is `connect`; its `cause` is the reason passed to `destroy(reason)`, or with no reason an `Error` whose message is `closed before connect`, and it carries no `connectionCause`. A reason is the caller's own, so one whose message or `code` reads like a TLS failure (the word `alert`, an `ERR_SSL_` code, `EPROTO`, a certificate verification code) is no longer sorted into `tls-handshake` or `tls-verify`: it is carried as the cause as it is. A TLS server sets no handshake time limit of its own: on Node.js the runtime's 120 second default applies, after which the socket is closed and `'tlsClientError'` fires with code `ERR_TLS_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`. Until then such a connection holds a `maxConnections` place, and `deadPeerTimeoutMs` does not cover it. On Bun and Deno we have not measured what bounds a handshake. The one change in behaviour: a caller who read a `tls-handshake` or `tls-verify` cause off a `connect()` they ended with their own `destroy(reason)` now sees no `connectionCause`, and tells that case apart by `err.cause === reason`.
