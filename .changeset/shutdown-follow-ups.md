**A shutdown now reports every acknowledgement it could not send, and never reports one as socket backpressure when the truth is a closed connection.** When a sender half-closes, its end ends our side of the socket too, and for a moment the connection still reads as open; a handler that settled in that moment had its ACK written to a socket that could no longer send it, and reported as `ACK dropped: socket backpressure`. The server now reads the socket itself: the ACK is not written, and it is reported as `ACK not sent: the connection closed before the handler settled`, with or without a `close()` in progress. A connection already waiting after a fatal framing error when `server.close()` (or `Connection.close()`) is called now makes the shutdown report too: the work abandoned when that wait ends by its bound, by the server's `drainTimeoutMs`, or by the sender closing is counted in an `MllpConnectionError` with `phase: 'close'` on `'error'`, where before nothing was reported. On a client, an error re-surfaced from a subscriber that threw now carries the `connectionId` of the connection, as every other client error does, so the payload is always `{ connectionId, error }`. Nothing a caller must change.
