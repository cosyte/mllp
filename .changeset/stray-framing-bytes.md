**A frame with a stray `FS` or `VT` inside it is refused whole, so no message in it is lost or left unacknowledged without a word.** Two messages joined by a bare `FS` inside one frame used to reach a default server's handler as the first message, after which the reader threw on the second and the connection closed before the first could be acknowledged; the sender resent it, and the second was never read. Joined by a `VT`, the first was dropped and the second handed to the handler and committed under an `AE`. Now `FrameReader` delivers nothing from such a frame and throws an `MllpFramingError` coded `MLLP_PAYLOAD_CONTAINS_VT` (a `VT` inside a frame, whatever the tolerances) or `MLLP_PAYLOAD_CONTAINS_FS` (with `allowFsOnly`, an `FS` that no `CR`, `LF` or next `VT` follows), whose `byteOffset` is the stray byte's own offset, whose `snippet` is that one framing byte and whose `message` holds no payload byte. On a server or a client that arrives on `'error'` with `connectionCause: 'framing-fatal'`, and the connection closes with no acknowledgement sent; the outcome is the same however the frame is split across reads. An `FS` followed by the next frame's `VT` is still two frames, both delivered and acknowledged in order. What a caller sees: frames that used to be delivered as a fragment (`MLLP_TRAILING_BYTES`, answered `AE` with the `nack` reason `'discarded-bytes'`), or delivered with the byte after a bare `FS` dropped, now close the connection with one of those codes instead; `MLLP_TRAILING_BYTES` and `'discarded-bytes'` stay in their types but are no longer raised or sent. What a caller may change: alert on the two codes on the `'error'` event rather than on `MLLP_TRAILING_BYTES`. From `@cosyte/mllp/arbitraries`, `messyStream()` no longer generates a frame with a `VT` or a bare `FS` inside it, since no reader accepts one.
